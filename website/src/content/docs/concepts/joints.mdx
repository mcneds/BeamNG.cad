The compiler combines the constraints between each pair of owned moving members, determines which relative motions remain, and proposes a supported joint.

<MateExplorer />

**How the conversion works.** `NativeMateScanner.cs` captures persistent native
geometry and assembly-space coordinates on the CAD thread. `joint_authoring.py`
builds a relative six-DOF constraint matrix from point/axis/plane geometry and
uses its nullspace plus geometric checks to propose supported motion. Mate
signatures preserve approval only while the captured constraints stay unchanged.
Role ownership and `joint_contract.py` reject wrong-group points, off-axis hinge
pairs, incompatible motion and unused physical constraints. `authored_shafts.py`
walks a connected, unbranched chain of supported joints. No mesh bound or source
body order supplies a missing hardpoint.

`translating_rack.py` fits the two members' positive masses independently to
their CAD mass/COM/inertia. Three parallel capped rails constrain rack rotation
and transverse movement; opposed steering hydros translate it, and both tie rods
keep fixed lengths. Their springs share the assembled numerical step budget.
The implementation derives the hydro's control sign by solving both front linkages
with a small positive and negative rack displacement. Positive steering input
must turn both wheels right in vehicle coordinates. This handles racks ahead of
or behind the axle and either axis direction; contradictory wheel responses stop
generation. The sign and toe gains are recorded in the steering contract.
The implementation converts physical rack speed to relative hydro rate using the
actuator's rest length, preserving speed when that numerical length changes.
See [BeamNG's hydro controls](https://documentation.beamng.com/modding/vehicle/sections/hydros/).
The implementation applies both actuators at one midpoint node between the rack
endpoints, with opposite housing supports and clearance for the full stroke.
The supports share the former total requested holding stiffness and damping;
they target the same physical translation with opposite length changes. A
single-sided numerical support introduced transverse-to-steering coupling even
in a symmetric CAD rack. A regression independently solves the exported static
stiffness and requires symmetric transverse loads to leave the rack center's
axial position unchanged. All support nodes receive redistributed CAD member
mass, and the assembled stiffness-and-damping step limit still applies. The
three guides use this assembled budget without an additional local frequency
cap. Native road-load verification remains in progress.
Compact rigid housings double mounting-node priority relative to their existing
CAD mass allocation, then refit the same CAD mass, COM and inertia. Other nodes
retain their relative allocation priority, including torque reaction supports.
All nodes remain positive and the achieved allocation is reported. This is a
numerical support policy; it neither adds a fixed 2 kg to each mount nor
calibrates the strength of a real fastener. Normal constraints share the full
assembled step budget; deformable collision cells retain an additional local cap.
The motion reference predicts the wheel linkage using the recorded native hydro
target, so a drifting rack cannot validate itself by supplying its own measured
position as the target. Every declared actuator must compile once and record
the same physical target; missing or conflicting targets reject the recording.
Earlier single-actuator isolated sweeps at ±10/±40 mm passed with peak
tracking errors 0.11/0.43 mm. Those recordings do not qualify the opposed-support
revision. Full-vehicle road-load acceptance of this new rack
still has to run on each generated vehicle.

`attachment_contract.py` creates three short directional connector beams per
breakable fastener, with stable release groups scoped to the child. Permanent
frame restraints distribute force through the calibrated frame supports. Each panel retains its
own nodes, triangles and flexbody binding after release. The boundary audit
rejects undeclared beams, torsionbars, triangles or rails that could keep a panel
attached, and rejects a skin bound to another part's nodes. The harness records
both deliberate cuts and force-ramp failures; independent mounts retain the
panel until their own connectors fail. Named linked sets release together.
Numerical stability caps and CAD mass fitting do not calibrate hardware rupture.

**Supported scope.** Native point, concentric/locked-concentric and compound hinge
fixtures were checked in SolidWorks, including reopen identity. Companion split,
source refresh and body reclassification were checked on owned native geometry.
The candidate also passed a fresh 36-group companion lifecycle with ten moving
members, save/reopen reference checks and clean owned SolidWorks shutdown. Member
splitting copies replacement parts directly into the final revision, avoiding
temporary assembly windows. Body/reference lookup caches last only for one
revision update, so a later source change cannot reuse stale CAD references.
The current conversion does not run a native CAD finite-motion sweep and does
not convert generic mate travel/angle limits or arbitrary linkages into physical
stops. Those constraints are rejected rather than discarded; use the supported
template's explicit travel/coilover stop settings and mark a CAD-only limit
positioning-only after review. Rack checks used a fixed housing and analytic
moments; two authored vehicle graphs passed offline mass/release checks. New
vehicles remain diagnostic until their own live verification passes.
